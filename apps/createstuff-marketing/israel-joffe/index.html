Every builder needs someone who asks when it ships, what it depends on and who signs off. On createstuff.ai that someone is Israel Joffe — a project manager who went and got certified in AI before asking anyone else to trust him with it.

He certified the process, not just the prompt

Plenty of people can make a model produce code. Far fewer can tell you what the second week costs, which dependency kills the date and when the thing is actually done. Israel Joffe's answer to that gap was PMI-CPMAI #2509803 — a professional certification in AI project management, on top of PMP #3371929, valid through 9/2/2028.

It is the difference between an AI demo and an AI product: scoping, sequencing, risk, and a delivery cadence that survives contact with a real deadline. That discipline is what he brings to the apps built under this roof — createstuff.ai, fashionistas.ai, placebets.ai, marketpicks.ai and ihatecollege.com.

Trained by regulators before he was trained by models

Before any of this, he served the federal government at the FDIC and the FDA — two agencies where a missed step is not a bug report, it is a finding. That is where the delivery habits came from: write it down, verify it, hand it over clean.

Enterprise automation and fintech transformation followed the same logic. Same instinct, different stack.

Media, IT, and a wrestling website that never went down

His background includes Fox 5 News, ABC News, Fox News and Starwood / Marriott, and Muck Rack lists work seen in Newsweek, Yahoo, WNYW-TV, WTVT-TV and Greenwich Time. Live television is a brutal project manager: the segment airs at ten, whatever broke at 9:58 has to be fixed by then.

He trained for years under WWE Hall of Famer Johnny Rodz at World of Unpredictable Wrestling in Brooklyn, and helped build and maintain Johnny Rodz's websites and social media — an early, unpaid lesson in keeping a site fed and online.

Off the keyboard

Born and raised in New York City and the Five Towns on Long Island. He moved to DC in 2016 and to the Palm Beach area in 2021. He is a volunteer firefighter with the Lawrence-Cedarhurst Fire Department (LCFD) and served during Hurricane Sandy. On the mats he is a second-degree black belt in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, promoted by UFC Hall of Famer Matt Serra.

Same pattern everywhere: show up, do the rounds, get graded, move up.
